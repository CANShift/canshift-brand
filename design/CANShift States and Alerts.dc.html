The severity grammar

The dash had one level, the critical takeover. Four levels need one promoted token: the amber already used for the DO NOT UNPLUG line during a firmware update now carries the warning role. No new colour beyond that, and the level is always carried by the rule and the ground, never by an icon or a badge.

Controls — the engaged states

anti-lag · traction · launch · pit limit · cruise

Every control reads the same four ways, so the driver never learns a second vocabulary. Off is a 2 px white outline on the page ground. Armed is the same outline pulsing at 1 s — the condition is met but the function has not fired. Active is a solid accent fill. Unavailable drops the outline to #333 and the text to #6b6b6b, and states the reason in the kicker. Armed and unavailable cells are marked in the column header rather than animated here.

WHY NO ICONS
A traction-control glyph is an OEM convention and unreadable at 320 px. The word plus the state carries it, and the word is what the driver would say out loud.
THE KICKER CARRIES THE REASON
A control that cannot arm never just greys out: the kicker states why — MOVING, EGT HIGH, NO WHEEL SPEED, BRAKE CUT. Outline #333, text #6b6b6b.
ACTIVE IS STEADY
The 1 s pulse belongs to armed and to armed only. An active control is a steady accent fill; traction control shows that it is intervening in the kicker (CUTTING), not by moving.

E

Control splash screens

800 ms takeover, then back to the page

When a control changes state the dash takes the whole screen for 800 ms, so the driver reads the change without looking for a small button. The grammar is fixed: a 2 px rule in the state's colour, the control name as kicker, the new state at 128 px, and the parameter that matters underneath. Engaging is accent, disengaging is ink, refusing is danger. No red field — that stays reserved for the critical alert. The page returns by an instant cut, and a second change inside the 800 ms replaces the splash rather than queueing.

WHEN A SPLASH IS EARNED
Only a state the driver asked for: engaging, disengaging, changing a level or a map, and a refusal. A value crossing a threshold is a warning on its widget, never a splash.
TIMING
800 ms for a change, 1.2 s for a refusal, both cut in and out with no transition. A new change replaces the splash and restarts the timer; splashes never queue. A critical alert preempts a splash immediately.
WHAT IT NEVER DOES
No red field, no pulse, no icon, no progress bar, and it never asks for a tap — the splash is a receipt, not a dialog. The page underneath keeps running and returns untouched.
F

Protection cuts

boost · fuel · ignition · knock · overheat · limp

A cut is the ECU protecting itself, not something the driver asked for — so it never earns a splash. It gets the cut band: a persistent full-width strip directly under the shift light, present for exactly as long as the cut is, naming what is being cut and why. The driver's question is always the same — why is the car down on power? — and the band answers it without hiding the page.

FROM THE TUNER
THE CUT VOCABULARY
BOOST CUT, FUEL CUT, IGNITION CUT, IGNITION RETARD, REV LIMIT, TRACTION CUT, PIT LIMIT CUT, OVERHEAT PROTECT, LIMP MODE. One name per cut, taken from the ECU profile, never invented at render time.
AMBER OR RED
Amber when the cut is holding a target — overboost, rev limit, traction, pit limit. Red when it is protecting the engine — oil pressure, overheat, limp. The value that caused it takes the same colour on its own widget.
TIMING
The band appears within one frame of the cut flag and stays for a 1.5 s minimum, so a 60 ms cut is still readable. The elapsed counter runs while cutting; a latched cut reads LATCHED instead. Two concurrent cuts stack, most severe on top, three maximum.
NOT A SPLASH
A cut is not driver-requested, so it never takes the screen and never pulses. If the underlying cause is dangerous, the critical alert follows on its own terms — the band does not become one.
